# Impeccable Design Context — Carlisle Contractor Portal

## Design Context

### Users

**Primary:** Roofing contractors in the field — on rooftops or job sites, one hand free, often gloved, in direct sunlight. Their job-to-be-done is fast: get to the right document, check an order status, or respond to an inspection before climbing back up. Speed and orientation are the entire UX. They are not browsing — they are executing.

**Secondary:** Carlisle Sales Reps reviewing quotes and inspection responses at a desk or on a tablet. Less urgent context, more deliberate. They need a clear view across all contractors' jobs.

**Context of use:** Bright sun on a phone screen, with gloves, under time pressure. White UIs are unreadable outdoors — glare defeats contrast. The dark theme is a functional decision, not aesthetic preference.

**Job to be done (primary):** Job → Product → Document in 3 taps or fewer.

---

### Brand Personality

> "The Cadillac of construction materials. Expensive, but the best of the best. High quality, reliable."

In 3 words: **precise · authoritative · durable**

This is not a rough construction app. It is the premium tier of the industry. Every surface should feel considered and engineered, not assembled. The closest physical analogy is the label inside a Carhartt jacket made in Detroit, or the stamped-metal spec plate on professional tool equipment. Expensive without being showy. Confident without being loud.

---

### Aesthetic Direction

**Theme:** Dark — not for atmosphere, but for function. Blue-tinted dark surfaces reduce glare and unify every screen toward the Carlisle brand identity. All backgrounds carry a blue undertone derived from the brand hue (#164da6). Pure charcoal (#1A1A1A) is not used.

**Color system (locked):**
```
Brand        #164da6   — Carlisle Blue. Logo, headers, structural anchors.
Interactive  #2E6FD8   — Buttons, active states, primary actions.
Highlight    #5B9BF0   — Focus rings, hover states, tab indicators.
Bg           #151C28   — App background (navy-black).
Surface      #1E2A3E   — Card surfaces.
Surface-alt  #243044   — Nested/secondary surfaces.
Nav          #0F1520   — Bottom nav and persistent chrome.
Amber        #F5A623   — Alerts, pending states, urgency (highest outdoor visibility).
Emerald      #4ACA6E   — Success, approved, delivered.
Error        #FF4C4C   — Failure, rejected, destructive only.
Text-sec     #A8B8D0   — Secondary text (blue-tinted, not pure gray).
Border       #2E3A52   — Card borders, dividers.
```
Red is not a brand color. It is reserved exclusively for failure/destructive states. Amber is the contrast partner to brand blue.

**Typography:**
- **Display / headings:** Big Shoulders Display — Wide, compressed, premium-industrial. Chicago heritage font that evokes manufacturing quality and American construction. Authoritative without being corporate.
- **UI / body:** Barlow — Designed for military/official contexts, extremely legible in data-dense UIs at small sizes. Carries none of the startup-font baggage. Clean without being sterile.
- **Minimum size:** 16px body. Bold (700) for all text below 18px. Minimum weight: 500 (medium) — thin strokes vanish in outdoor glare.
- **Labels and badges:** All-caps + letter-spacing (tracking-widest). Faster to scan than mixed case outdoors.

**References:**
- Elevate Technical App (Carlisle's own app — this should feel better than that)
- Angi's List — but more rugged, more premium, less consumer-facing softness

**Anti-references:**
- Generic Tailwind admin templates (same-card grids, cyan accents, identical spacing everywhere)
- Overly consumer-friendly apps (rounded-everything, pastel states, playful microcopy)
- Procore-style corporate heaviness (dense nav, overwhelming sidebar structure)

---

### Design Principles

1. **Premium over rugged.** This is not a rough tool — it is the Cadillac of contractor apps. Every surface should feel engineered, precise, and considered. If a design choice looks "good enough," keep pushing until it looks intentional.

2. **Speed is the offering.** The primary success metric is how quickly a contractor gets to the document they need. Navigation must never require thought. The most recent job, the most urgent alert, and the document library must be reachable in one or two taps from anywhere.

3. **Outdoor-first legibility.** Every type size, weight, and color contrast decision must pass a mental "direct sunlight on a phone" test. Minimum 52px tap targets. No outlined-only badges. Filled icons only. No thin-stroke anything.

4. **Authoritative hierarchy.** The most important information on any screen should be unmistakably obvious. Status should be readable at a glance. Secondary info recedes. Nothing competes.

5. **Purposeful motion only.** Transitions that orient (page entry, tab switch, accordion expand) are correct. Decorative animation is wrong in this context — it distracts from a time-pressured task. Grid-template-rows for expand/collapse. Opacity + translateY for entrances. No bounce, no elastic.

---

### Technical Constraints

- Vue 3 + Vite + Tailwind CSS v4 (with `@tailwindcss/vite` plugin)
- vue-i18n for all UI strings (en, es, fr, pt)
- Local JSON data (no API)
- Mobile-first: bottom nav with icons + labels
- Desktop: left sidebar nav using `--color-nav`
- `@` alias resolves to `./src`
- Outdoor Mode: CSS class on `<body>` via `useOutdoorMode` composable, persisted in localStorage
- WCAG AA minimum for text contrast. Status badges must be solid-fill (not outlined).
